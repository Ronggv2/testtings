  • Osaka

    • Osaka is Japan’s third-largest city and a vibrant hub of food, culture, and commerce. Known as “Japan’s Kitchen”, it's famous for its friendly people, street food, and lively atmosphere.

    • Osaka Castle (大阪城)

      • A historic symbol of Osaka, surrounded by beautiful parks and moats. Especially stunning during cherry blossom season.

    • Dotonbori District

      • The heart of Osaka nightlife with bright neon signs (like the Glico Man), restaurants, shops, and street performances.

  • Must Try Foods

    • Takoyaki

      • Octopus balls with sauce and bonito flakes.

    • Okonomiyaki

      • Savory pancake with cabbage, meat, seafood, and sauce.

    • Kushikatsu

      • Deep-fried skewered meat and veggies.

  • Why Visit Osaka?

    • It’s less formal than Tokyo, more relaxed, and known for its humor, energy, and warmth. Plus, it's a great base for day trips to Kyoto, Nara, and Kobe.

  • Kyoto

    • one of the most beautiful and historically rich cities in Japan:

  • Known For

    • Ancient temples, shrines, traditional tea houses, geisha, gardens, and seasonal beauty (especially cherry blossoms and autumn leaves)

  • History

    • Capital of Japan from 794 to 1868 (for over 1,000 years)

    • Fushimi Inari Taisha

      • Famous for its thousands of red torii gates that line mountain trails.

      Dedicated to the Shinto god of rice and prosperity.

    • Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

      • A peaceful bamboo forest with nearby attractions like the Togetsukyo Bridge and Monkey Park Iwatayama.

    • Kiyomizu-dera Temple

      • A UNESCO World Heritage Site with a massive wooden terrace overlooking the hills and city.

      Especially beautiful during sakura (cherry blossom) season and fall.

    • Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

      • A Zen temple in kyoto and the top two floors are completely cooverd in gold leaf.
